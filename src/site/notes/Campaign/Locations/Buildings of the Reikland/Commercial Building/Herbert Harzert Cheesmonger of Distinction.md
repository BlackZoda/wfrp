* [[Campaign/Hooks/Something Doesn't Smell Right\|Something Doesn't Smell Right]]

[[Herbert Harzert\|Herbert Harzert]] runs Harzet’s cheese shop on [[Altstrasse\|Altstrasse]] in [[World/The Empire/Electorial Provinces/Reikland/Vorbergland/Ubersreik/Ubersreik\|Ubersreik]]. He’s proud to call it ‘the finest cheese shop in Ubersreik’, although there is not much competion.

He’s recently branched out and has started stocking the “posh” [[World/Other Nations/Bretonnia/Bretonnia\|Bretonnian]] cheese that travel down the [[Grey Lady Pass\|Grey Lady Pass]]. Harzert prefers the harder, Empire cheeses, but pretends to love the new, softer, Bretonnian varieties to boost sales.

Property is relatively expensive on Altstrasse because it is so busy, and each shop is taxed by frontage. Therefore Harzert’s, like all the eighbouring shops, is long and thin. The shop floor is bifurcated by a broad, wooden counter. On one side, customers queue for service and cast their eyes and nose over the array of produce. On the other side, Harzert’s office is situated at the back of the ground floor. It’s a dishvelled space with a pair of desks and a small safe for keeping the takings overnight. Piles of correspondence from suppliers across the [[World/The Empire/Electorial Provinces/Reikland/Reikland\|Reikland]] and [[World/Other Nations/Bretonnia/Bretonnia\|Bretonnia]] are stacked haphazzardly everywhere.

Beneath the busy shop floor lies Harzert’s cold, damp cellar in chich he stores cheeses not yet entire ripe enough to serve. The first floor is where Herzert – a widower, who lost his young wife a year ago – lives alone. His apartment comprises a bedroom, a kitchen and a small parlour – not that he has many visitors: stinking cheese is not conducive to polite conversation. The top two floors are leased out to private tenants who reach their apartments via a ricketly staircase in the ally behind the shop. A large family – sixteen in total – live on the second floor. The formidable [[Frau Duschen\|Frau Duschen]], a widow, who lives cheek by jowl with her eleven children, three of their spouses, and two grandchildren. [[Gustav Klosch\|Gustav Klosch]], an artist, rents the top floor and endures the noise and cheesy reek for the quality of the light.
