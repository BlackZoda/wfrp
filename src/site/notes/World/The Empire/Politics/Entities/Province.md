There are also many lesser, non-[[World/The Empire/Politics/Entities/Electoral Province\|Electoral Province]]. The rulers of these Provinces are not considered to have royal blood, but are important for historical, political, religious, or societal reasons. As a result, non-[[World/The Empire/Politics/Titles/Elector\|Elector]] often rival, or nearly equal, the less powerful Electors.

Provincial rulers are responsible first to their liege, the relevant Elector above them, and secondly to the [[World/The Empire/Politics/Titles/Emperor\|Emperor]]. They appeal diretly to their Elector not the Emperor, for justice if they feel wronged. Equally, if Emperor [[World/The Empire/Politics/People/Emperor Karl-Franz I\|Emperor Karl-Franz I]] wishes to find fault with the behaviour of [[Chancellor Dachs og Ostermark\|Chancellor Dachs og Ostermark]], he first raises the matter with [[World/The Empire/Politics/People/Gustav XI\|Gustav XI]] ([[Grand Duke of Talabecland\|Grand Duke of Talabecland]]) who had better do something about the Emperor’s concerns. These non-[[World/The Empire/Politics/Entities/Electoral Province\|Electoral Province]], being under an Elector, are effectively covered by the same immunities and exemptions as the “parent” Electoral Province. This need not be a bad thing.

Just to add to the confusion, some of the [[World/The Empire/Politics/Entities/Province\|Province]] are also ruled directly by an [[World/The Empire/Politics/Titles/Elector Count\|Elector Count]].
