Most [[World/The Empire/Politics/Entities/Town\|Town]]s are ruled by a [[Liege Lord\|Liege Lord]], either the overall ruler of the [[World/The Empire/Politics/Entities/Province\|Province]] or a lesser [[Rules/Career/Noble\|Noble]] sworn to the service of that ruler. Some are Freistadts or *”free towns”* that hold a [[Charter\|Charter]] fom and [[World/The Empire/Politics/Titles/Elector Count\|Elector Count]] or the [[World/The Empire/Politics/Titles/Emperor\|Emperor]] and answer only to them.

[[World/The Empire/Electorial Provinces/Reikland/Settlements/Kemperbad\|Kemperbad]] in the [[World/The Empire/Electorial Provinces/Reikland/Reikland\|Reikland]] is one of the largest Freistadts and, like most free towns, is ruled by a council drawn from the senior members of the town’s [[World/The Empire/Politics/Institutions/Guild\|Guild]]s and [[Cult\|Cult]]s. Prosperous towns pay well for a charter, and many cash-strapped rulers have taken advantage of this easy source of funds.

Independent towns can be thorn in the flesh of all higher authorities, since a noble’s agents cannot normally collect [[Taxes\|Taxes]] or raise [[Troops\|Troops]] from them, depending upon the charter’s terms. Local nobels are always looking for an excuse to have a town’s charter revoked and have themselves installed as its rulers.

In the past, some free towns have been known to be powerful enough to wage war against Provincial rulers, and powerful enough to be worth a private war waged by a would-be ruler. Following a disastrous harvest, the [[Freistadt of Streissen\|Freistadt of Streissen]] suffered from bread riots in the year [[World/The Empire/Politics/People/Emperor Karl-Franz I\|Emperor Karl-Franz I]] ascended the throne. [[Grand Countess Lumila of Averland\|Grand Countess Lumila of Averland]] made it quite clear that she would only send her troops to keep the peace after the town’s government agreed to pass all its privileges and authority over to her. The massacre that accompanied her takeover is one of the most infamous chapters of recent history.
