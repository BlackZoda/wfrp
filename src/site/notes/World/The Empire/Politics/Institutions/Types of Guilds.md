There are guilds for just about every trade, craft, and profession imaginable. The [[Craft Guild\|Craft Guild]]s ([[Metalworker\|Metalworker]]s, [[Carpenter\|Carpenter]]s, [[Cartwright\|Cartwright]]s, [[Mason\|Mason]]s, [[Jeweller\|Jeweller]]s, [[Tailor\|Tailor]]s, and the like) contro standards of workmanship. Every apprentice is examined by a guild official before being granted [[Journeyman\|Journeyman]] status, with the right to work without a master. The craft guilds also require that any craftsman claiming to have served an [[Apprenticeship\|Apprenticeship]] elsewhere must present them with testimonals and letters of introduction from any previous [[World/The Empire/Politics/Institutions/Guild\|Guild]] they had joined.

The [[Labour Guilds\|Labour Guilds]] (for example, [[Labourer\|Labourer]]s, [[Teamster\|Teamster]]s, and [[Rules/Career/Stevedore\|Stevedore]]s) are not so strict in their requirements, since same standards of craftsmanship do not apply to their work. However, they do keep close control of their members’ activities and protect their labour monopolies ruthlessly.

The [[Merchants’ Guild\|Merchants’ Guild]]s works in much the same as a Craft Guild, overseeing apprenticeships and so on, but its members are free to seek business as they please. The majority of mercantile operations are family-based, and outside these families new guild members are not encouraged. Occasionally, the guild may intervene when competition or disputes between members threaten its unity or reputation bu, by and large, the Merchants’ Guild is content to let members conduct business as they see fit.

All guild members pay an annual membership fee, which helps to maintain the guilds’ premises. These range from lavish town-houses to well concealed, rambling hideaways, to small rooms at the back of an artisan’s shop. Apprentices must pay a fee in order to be examined by a guild official before progressing to full membership and being allowed to ply their trade in a town. In some places, members of craft guilds are also expected to do one day’s work per year for the guild, for which they are not paid.

Those foolish enough to operate without the backing of a guild can expect all kinds of trouble: a verbal warning, followed by threats of physical voilence – which are swiftly carried out if the person concerned continues to be recalcitrant.
