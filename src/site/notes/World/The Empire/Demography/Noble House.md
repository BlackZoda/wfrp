The Empire’s nobility ranges from the mighty [[Imperial House\|Imperial House]] [[Holswig-Schliestein\|Holswig-Schliestein]] to the many lowly [[Lesser Houses\|Lesser Houses]] comprising the [[Baronet\|Baronet]]s and [[Rules/Career/Knight\|Knight]]s found on every estate. The imprtance and wealth attached to apperently similar titles varies greatly. For example, the [[Grand Countess of Averland\|Grand Countess of Averland]] rules a vast area lying between the river [[Aver\|Aver]], the [[Upper Reik\|Upper Reik]], and the [[Black Mountains\|Black Mountains]], while the [[Countess of Bylor\|Countess of Bylor]] rules a Sylvian bog in Eastern [[Stirland\|Stirland]] that would barely support half of a poor baronet of the [[World/The Empire/Electorial Provinces/Reikland/Reikland\|Reikland]]. This diversity is noticeable in the wealth of the more important families, particularly among the [[Royal Houses\|Royal Houses]] who hold [[World/The Empire/Politics/Titles/Elector\|Elector]]al positions. The members of the wealthy families are alway recognisable by their rich robes and glittering jewels. It’s a popular saying that, on a moonless night, the sparkling [[Imperial Court\|Imperial Court]] at [[World/The Empire/Electorial Provinces/Reikland/Altdorf/Altdorf\|Altdorf]] can be seen as far away as [[World/The Empire/Electorial Provinces/Wissenland/Nuln\|Nuln]]. In contrast, the nobles of the eastern Empire often have difficulty convincing visitors that they are, in fact, of noble blood.

## Titles
The [[World/The Empire/Politics/Titles/Emperor\|Emperor]] is known as [[His Imperial Majesty\|His Imperial Majesty]], the [[World/The Empire/Politics/People/Emperor Karl-Franz I\|Emperor Karl-Franz I]]. Rulers of a [[Principality\|Principality]] are styled as [[Prince\|Prince]] or [[Princess\|Princess]], while rulers of a [[Duchy\|Duchy]] are known as [[Duke\|Duke]] or [[Duchess\|Duchess]]. A ruler of a [[County\|County]] are known as [[Count\|Count]] or [[Countess\|Countess]], and rulers of a [[Barony\|Barony]] are known as [[Baron\|Baron]] or [[Baronesse\|Baronesse]], as are heirs to Counties and Duchies. Rulers of a [[March\|March]] (borderlands) are called [[Magrave\|Magrave]]. Heirs to Baronies are known as [[Baronet\|Baronet]]s, and heirs to Principalities are known as [[Crown Prince\|Crown Prince]]s. The ruler of the [[League of Ostermark\|League of Ostermark]] is known as the [[Chancellor\|Chancellor]].

However, local customs ensure that no two parts of [[World/The Empire/The Empire\|The Empire]] have a consistent standard, and that there are many more titles than the few already listed. In [[Nordland\|Nordland]], heirs to costal estates are titled [[Master\|Master]] or [[Mistress\|Mistress]]. Doen the [[World/The Empire/Rivers and Canals/Reik\|Reik]], many [[World/The Empire/Politics/Entities/Prime Estate\|Prime Estate]]s are ruled by [[Reikgraf\|Reikgraf]]sand [[Reikgravin\|Reikgravin]]s. In many parts of [[World/The Empire/The Empire\|The Empire]], [[Hochadel Noble House\|Hochadel Noble House]]s, those who have been counted as noble for over 1000 years, use the title [[Graf\|Graf]] and [[Gravin\|Gravin]] rather than [[Duke\|Duke]] and [[Duchess\|Duchess]] or [[Count\|Count]] and [[Countess\|Countess]]. That said, in [[World/The Empire/Electorial Provinces/Middenland/Middenland\|Middenland]] they prefer the title [[Grafina\|Grafina]] to [[Gravin\|Gravin]], which they see as somewhat effete.

In general the prefix [[Grand\|Grand]] signifies that the title holder is also an [[World/The Empire/Politics/Titles/Elector\|Elector]] – thus [[World/The Empire/Politics/People/Emperor Karl-Franz I\|Emperor Karl-Franz I]] is also the [[Grand Prince of Reikland\|Grand Prince of Reikland]]. However, not all rulers choose to use it: [[Graf Boris Todbringer of Middenheim\|Graf Boris Todbringer of Middenheim]], for eample, is not styled [[Grand Graf\|Grand Graf]] or [[Grand Duke\|Grand Duke]] largely because he doesn’t like the way either title sound. Instead, he is universally known as “the Graf”.
