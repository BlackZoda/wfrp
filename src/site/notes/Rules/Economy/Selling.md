Selling your trappings during desperate times, or simply offloading loot, is handled in exactly the same fashion as [[Buying\|Buying]], but this time you’ll be making money, not spending it. So, you first check for [[Rules/Economy/Availability\|Availability]] of a buyer in the same fashion as checking for stock. You then bargain and trade, with [[Rules/Skills/Evaluate\|Evaluate]] Tests and [[Rules/Skills/Haggle\|Haggle]] Tests as the GM prefers, and finally you set a price. Typically, the base cost when selling is *half the item’s listed price*, meaning when selling second-hand, you usually make between a quarter to half an item’s worth after [[Rules/Skills/Haggle\|Haggle]]ing.

If you have a Career like [[Rules/Career/Fence\|Fence]] or [[Rules/Career/Merchant\|Merchant]], and you put time into [[Rules/Skills/Gossip\|Gossip]] Tests and wandering the local town, you might find someone willing to pay up to 80% of the item’s listed value, though that is left in the hands of the GM.

## Lowering the Price
If you can’t find a buyer, you can try lowering the price. Each time you half the money you are willing to accept, the [[Rules/Economy/Availability\|Availability]] of a buyer increses by one step.

## Bartering
Rural and mercantile commerce often involves trading one commodity for another without exchanging money. To resolve barttering of this type, compare the [[Rules/Economy/Availability\|Availability]] of the items being traded against those being acquired. The barter ratio indicates how many “units” must be traded, and how many “units” of the other commodity are acquired. “Units” are defined by checking the equipment list prices of both items and grouping them into packages of roughly equivalent value.
