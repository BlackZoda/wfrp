Magisters who channel [[World/Magic/Azyr\|Azyr]] are astromancers, also known as [[Celestial Wizards\|Celestial Wizards]] og ‘Blue Wizards’ by the uneducated. They favour skullcaps, high-collared cloaks, and expensive robes with deep blue and blacks, emblazoned with silver and gold motifs to represent the night sky. Somehere on their person they wear the symbol of their order, the [[Comet of Power\|Comet of Power]]. Many present a well-groomed, aloof appearance to the world.

Each magister carries  staff, usually topped ith an ornamental telescope, miniature orrery, or armillary sphere. Sometimes these are funtional, but they primarily serve as a symbolic badge of the Astromancer’s art.

Progression through the ranks of the Celestial College is orderly, like the arrangement of the night sky. After passing rigorous exams, new apprentices (called acolytes) are expected to serve their master without complaing – taking observations, readying instruments, fetching tomes, uncovering research, and undertaking any menial task that the astromancer considers beneath them.

The most perilous and humilitaing chore is cleaning the college’s glass domes of bird droppings. When their master does not want to travel, acolytes may be sent long distance errands to check an obscure detail from a premonition or to witness a meteorological phenomenon.
## Ascendant Constellations
Once an acolyte becomes a full astromancer, they receive a cermonial telescope from the [[High Celestial\|High Celestial]] and freedom to pursue their own research. Those who stay in the college may be involved in the [[Grand Experiment\|Grand Experiment]] or spend their time politicking for a study higher in the towers. Other astromancers are wanderers, exploring the world for ne perspectives on the heavens or rumours of portents in distant lands. Some sell their talents to sea captains in exchange for passage to foreign parts. Astromancers are valued advisors to the powerful and some establish themselves in a noble’s retinue.

The first astromancers were stargazers, astrologers, and those who unconsciously manipulted the [[Blue Wind\|Blue Wind]]. [[Teclis\|Teclis]] helped them channel their abilities into the [[Lore of Heavens\|Lore of Heavens]], a body of sorcery later expanded by human magisters to encompass spells to harness the airy element, discern fate, and change to their will.

Divination amongst astromancers is a combination of magical prowess, mathematics, and close interpretation of portents. Every member of the [[Celestial Order\|Celestial Order]] scrutinises the heavens for a glimpse of the future, but some also use other means. Crystal balls are said to focus [[World/Magic/Azyr\|Azyr]] and make it easier to read. Astromancers often use the movement of air itself, by interpreting the patterns of wind on water, the saying of trees or the drift of fine ashes when thrown into the air.

The typical astromancer is an intellectual with their head almost literally in the clouds. They use their impressive powers of reason to untangle the unknowable, which keeps them divorced from trivial day-to-day matters. They live for study, riting treatises on magical theory, astronomy meteorology, and prognostication.

Celestial Wizards tend to arrogance and petty academic spats proliferate. Prognostication is lucrative and many astromancers are wealthy, spending their riches on fine, ostentatious robes and elaborate astronomical and magical instruments.
## Relationships
### [[The Imperial Navy\|The Imperial Navy]]
[[The Imperial Sea Lords\|The Imperial Sea Lords]] have long cultivated an association with astromancers, who are much in demand for their ability to read the night sky, control the weather, and foresee perils at sea. The [[World/The Empire/Electorial Provinces/Reikland/Altdorf/Colleges of Magic/The Celestial College\|The Celestial College]] have leveraged this to fuel their appetite for exploration. Astromancers join ship’s companies in exchange for the Navy furnishing expeditions.

[[Lord Xavier Köhler\|Lord Xavier Köhler]] is a Celestial Wizard who holds high naval rank, known as the [[Transcriber of Wind\|Transcriber of Wind]] for his preternatural ability to forsee and change the weather. He fought with [[World/The Empire/Politics/People/Emperor Karl-Franz I\|Emperor Karl-Franz I]] at the [[Battle of Nordland Fjord\|Battle of Nordland Fjord]] and now advises his older brother, [[Imperial Sea Lord Baron Ludolf Köhler of Dietershafen\|Imperial Sea Lord Baron Ludolf Köhler of Dietershafen]].
### The Cult of Verena
Astromancers are scholars who value the acquisition of knowledge, so they have a natural affinity with the [[World/Religion and Belief/The Cults/Cult of Verena\|Cult of Verena]]. More than one Celestial magister has granted benefactions or bequeathed his library to a temple and the relationship is mostly harmonious. Some priests detect a faint condescension from the astromancers, tainted by a suspicion that wizards lack reverence for the divine. The influential Verenan abbot [[Gregor Zweifler\|Gregor Zweifler]] writes letters to high priests across [[World/The Empire/The Empire\|The Empire]], warning that the astromancers have taken to worshipping planets over gods and that the cult must take action.
### The Cult of Morr
The [[World/Religion and Belief/The Cults/Cult of Morr\|Cult of Morr]] is split on the matter of astromancy. The [[God of Dreams\|God of Dreams]] is not a jealous deity and his augurs in [[World/The Empire/The Empire\|The Empire]] occasionally consult with the college for an expert opinion when they receive troubling portents. However, hard-line Morrians outside [[World/The Empire/The Empire\|The Empire]] preach that unless precognition comes from [[World/Religion and Belief/Gods/Morr\|Morr]], it is inspired by [[The Great Conspirator\|The Great Conspirator]]. Recently the unveiling of the [[Thaumodivinator\|Thaumodivinator]], a fortune telling machine designed by wizards of the [[The Celestial Order\|The Celestial Order]], has led to increased tensions with the [[World/Religion and Belief/The Cults/Cult of Morr\|Cult of Morr]].
