Your [[Rules/Status/Status\|Status]] is directy linked to the amount of money you earn while working. The higher your [[Rules/Status/Status\|Status]], the more money you can earn as your good name attracts business. If the GM agrees, during play you can spend a week to work in your [[Rules/Career/Career\|Career]] assuming you are in a place where such is feasble (it’s hard to be a [[Rules/Career/Watchman\|Watchman]] in the middle of a wateland). This is called [[Earning\|Earning]].

When Earning, make an **Aberage (+20) [[Rules/Tests/Dramatic Test\|Dramatic Test]]** (WFRP page 152) against the [[Earning\|Earning]] [[Rules/Skills/Skill\|Skill]] of your [[Rules/Career/Career\|Career]] (the Skill marked in italics in the Career’s first level). If passed, you receive that money marked in the table below. If failed, you receive half the money. If an [[Astounding Failure\|Astounding Failure]] (-6) is scored, you have a very bad week, and earn nothing (or have your money stolen, or some similar mishap).

This total is not stricktly speaking how much money you can earn, it’s more a representation of how much money you have left at the end of the week after all your expenses are taken into account.

**Note:** This is the same amount of coin earned with an [[Rules/Between Adventures/General Endeavours/Income\|Income]] [[Endeavour\|Endeavour]] (WFRP page 198).

**Exampe:** *Gunther the Coachman decides he’ll work for the Four Seasons Coaching House for a week while his peers research a library for things he frankly doesn’t understand, and secretly thinks may be heretical if he did. The GM agrees to let Gunther enact some Earning, so Gunther rolls against his Drive (Coach) skill (his Earning Skill for Coachman) and passes. Given his Status is Silver 2, he can roll 2d10 and earn that much silver. Unfortunately, he only rolls a 1 and a 2 for a total of 3 silver shillings; clearly Gunther was drinking a lot after his long work shifts, and has little coin remaining…*
